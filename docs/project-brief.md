## 1. Goal

An ESP32-based Wi-Fi "modem" in a 3D-printed replica of a 1990s US Robotics Sportster 33.6 Faxmodem. It has a working DB25 RS232 port and is driven by the Hayes AT command set. It must work with a modern PC (terminal emulator + USB-to-serial adapter) and with genuine historic machines that have a real serial port (e.g. Amiga).

Behaviour on dial:
`ATDT<number>` → dial tone → DTMF of the digits actually dialled → TCP connection → handshake sound for the emulated speed → `CONNECT <speed>` → Telnet/raw passthrough to the remote host (usually a BBS).
On failure → busy tone + result code.

**Design priority (Jon, 20 Oct):** future-proof the design as much as possible. Jon is willing to have Claude port Zimodem to the ESP32-C3, C6 or S3 if that gives better performance and expansion potential.

References:
- Overall project similar to: https://github.com/caiot5/retroexternalmodem (Raspberry Pi + tcpser, GPL-3.0)
- Firmware base: https://github.com/bozimmerman/Zimodem (Bo Zimmerman, Apache-2.0)
- Case: "RetroExternalModem" by luizpacheco, https://www.thingiverse.com/thing:7063155 (CC BY-NC)

## 4. Workstream: 3D Case Design

### 4.1 Existing model (measured from the STLs, 20 Oct 2026)
- Files are in **`3d-model/`** (not `case/`): `USRRasp-v5-Top.stl`, `-Bottom.stl`, `-LockSpk.stl` (Ø42.5 × 3 mm ring), `-LockAmp.stl` (10 × 40 × 3 mm), `-VolDial.stl` (Ø18 × 11.5 mm), `-TabRJ45-20.stl`, `-TabUSB-20.stl`, plus `USR Modem.3mf` (Bambu Studio project, same unmodified STLs on plates). Render images of each part are in `images/`.
- All meshes are watertight except `VolDial` (needs repair before boolean edits). Top and Bottom share one assembly coordinate frame, so they can be edited together.
- STL only, no parametric source. Jon wants **Claude to edit the meshes directly** (with accurate dimensions) rather than give Fusion instructions.
- A first print is done (Bambu P1S, all Bambu Beige PLA). Print quality is very high and lid and base fit, but **would benefit from alignment features** (4.4). The 4-colour AMS is available, but the case will print entirely in beige.

## 5. Workstream: Electronics

### 5.1 RS232: full signal set required (DB25 female, DCE)
Modem outputs (drivers): **RXD (3), CTS (5), DSR (6), DCD (8), RI (22)**. Modem inputs (receivers): **TXD (2), RTS (4), DTR (20)**. GND is pin 7. Pin numbers are DB25.
- **Wiring rule:** connect only DB25 pins 2–8, 20 and 22. Leave all other pins unconnected (not grounded). Amiga serial ports carry supply voltages on some non-standard pins, and fully wired straight cables are common.
- **Bench prototype connector (Jon, 22 Oct): DB9 female (MP8022), wired as DCE** using the standard DB9 pinout: **1 DCD, 2 RXD, 3 TXD, 4 DTR, 5 GND, 6 DSR, 7 RTS, 8 CTS, 9 RI**. The Unitek adapter (DB9M, DTE) plugs straight in, with no null-modem. The final build uses the DB25 only (pins above).
- **Decided (20 Oct): 2× MAX3232 DIP-16** (ePartners SP0007, MAX3232EPE). MAX3238 dropped: no NZ distributor.
  - 2 chips = 4 drivers + 4 receivers. Drivers: **RXD, CTS, DCD, RI**. Receivers: **TXD, RTS, DTR** (1 spare).
  - **DSR is hard-wired permanently asserted**, matching the real Sportster's factory default (`&S0`, DSR always on). Firmware can't control DSR. The exact method is set in the schematic.
  - Each chip needs 5 × 0.1 µF ceramic capacitors at 3.3 V (4 charge-pump + 1 bypass), so 10 in total.
  - Powered from 3.3 V, so TTL levels match the S3 directly.
  - At $0.99 the chips may not be genuine. Buy spares, fit them in DIP sockets, and bench-check each at 3.3 V: V+ should read about +5.5 V and V− about −5.5 V.

### 5.2 Microcontroller
GPIO budget with every LED on its own pin (*Proposed*, see 5.4): 7 serial (TXD, RXD, RTS, CTS, DTR, DCD, RI; DSR is hard-wired, 5.1) + 3 I2S (BCLK, LRC, DIN) + 1 ADC (volume pot) + 7 LEDs + 1 amp shutdown (SD) = **19** (no reset button, Jon 22 Oct).
Sharing LEDs with signal lines was dropped: the RD/SD "stretched" behaviour needs firmware control, and loading the transceiver's receiver outputs with LEDs degrades the logic level.
Flash: OTA needs two app slots. Zimodem's reference ESP32 build uses 8 MB flash with 3 MB app space, so 8 MB+ is required for comfortable OTA plus sound storage.

**Board — *Proposed (revised 20 Oct)*: ESP32-S3 N16R8 DevKitC-style board** (ePartners ST8002, assembled). Reasons:
- **Memory:** 16 MB flash (OTA + full handshake sound set with room to spare) and 8 MB PSRAM (large TCP/audio buffers, SSH or multiple connections later). The C6 has no external PSRAM support at all.
- **CPU:** dual-core 240 MHz. Audio playback can run on one core while Wi-Fi, serial and Telnet run on the other. The C6 and C3 are single-core 160 MHz.
- **GPIO:** ample for the 20-pin budget plus future expansion.
- **Native USB OTG:** enables a future option of presenting the modem as a USB CDC serial port, so a modern PC needs no USB-serial adapter (Q25). The C6 and C3 have USB Serial/JTAG only.

- **Why not C6:** its advantages (Wi-Fi 6 on 2.4 GHz, Thread/Zigbee/Matter) give nothing at modem data rates, and the ePartners listing is unreliable.
- **Why not classic ESP32:** the earlier proposal (avoid porting) is outweighed now that Jon accepts a port, and none of the ePartners classic boards confirms 8 MB+ flash.

### 5.3 Audio
- MAX98357A on I2S, powered from 5 V. At full scale it can deliver over 1 W into 8 Ω, more than any of the candidate speakers can take.
- **Firmware hard volume cap (required):** a fixed maximum sample amplitude in code, applied after the pot and `ATL` scaling, so no setting can exceed the speaker rating. **Speaker decided 22 Oct: BZ0512, 0.25 W → cap ≈ −7 dB below full scale** (≈45 % amplitude). For reference, 0.5 W would be ≈ −4 to −5 dB and 1 W (AS3006 on the bench) ≈ −1 to −2 dB. Set the cap as a single constant per speaker and confirm by ear (no distortion at maximum) on the bench.
- *Proposed:* GAIN pin via **100 kΩ to Vin = 3 dB** (lowest setting; floating = 9 dB). Solder the resistor directly between the module's GAIN and Vin pads.
- ***Proposed* mounting (23 Oct): the amp module stays a separate board, mounted near the speaker** (Q68). It connects to the S3 board (Board A, 5.6) with a **6-wire lead**: Vin (5 V), GND, BCLK, LRC, DIN, SD. Keep the lead ≲20 cm, with GND next to BCLK. The speaker leads solder to the module's +/− pads (or fit a 2-pin 5 mm terminal block). Reasons: lying flat on Board B it would cover ≈7 of its 30 rows, which the two MAX3232s need; standing upright it is too tall (≈22 mm above the board top, ≈31 mm above the feet, against 29 mm clear height under the lid); and a separate module keeps all the audio wiring on Board A.
- *Proposed:* **SD pin driven by a GPIO**. Low = shutdown (removes idle hiss/pops); 3.3 V high = on, left channel.
- The MAX98357A has no analogue volume input. The volume pot (**WH148 B10K, 10 kΩ linear, decided**, 4.4) is wired as a divider into an **ESP32 ADC1 pin** (100 nF at the wiper) and the firmware scales the volume.
- **Pot value is not critical (20 Oct):** any **linear (B) pot from about 5 kΩ to 100 kΩ** works. It draws 3.3 V ÷ R from the 3.3 V rail (10 kΩ ≈ 0.33 mA, 50 kΩ ≈ 0.07 mA, both negligible). Higher values give a higher source impedance into the ADC, but the 100 nF wiper capacitor supplies the ADC's sampling charge, so readings stay stable up to ~100 kΩ. Prefer **linear** taper: the firmware applies the audio (log) curve itself; a log pot would also work but makes the curve calibration less predictable. **So choose the pot on mechanical fit (body, bush, shaft) for the Case design.**
- No SD card.

### 5.5 Power (revised 23 Oct: USB-C power input)
- **Power input (Jon, 23 Oct, Q65): a USB-C socket on the rear panel**, replacing the 2.1 mm DC jack. It is a **panel-mount USB-C socket with a 2-wire pigtail (+ and GND), bought from Temu; no part number.** Supply: **Raspberry Pi USB-C power supply, 3 A**, measured **5.26 V** on the pigtail wires (meter, no load). Estimated peak draw is ≈0.7 A (S3 Wi-Fi transmit ≈0.5 A, amp ≈0.15 A at the BZ0512 volume cap, MAX3232s and LEDs ≈0.05 A), well inside 3 A.
  - **CC pull-downs (5.1 kΩ from each CC pin to GND):** a USB-C-to-C supply that follows the USB-C rules sends no power until it sees them. The Raspberry Pi supply outputs 5 V without checking, so **Jon's 5.26 V reading doesn't show whether this socket has them** (they would be inside the socket moulding if fitted). **With the Raspberry Pi supply it doesn't matter.** It only matters if another USB-C charger (e.g. a phone charger with a C-to-C cable) is ever used: test once (bench check 6). If it gives 0 V, the socket has no pull-downs; then either stick to the Raspberry Pi supply (or any USB-A charger with an A-to-C cable), or replace the socket with one that has them. **Decided (Jon, 23 Oct): leave it as is; Jon's supplies work with it.**
  - The pigtail's two wires go to the main board's power header, through the power switch. The USB data lines aren't brought out, so there is nothing else to connect.
- **Reverse-polarity protection: removed (23 Oct).** A USB-C plug can't be reversed onto VBUS/GND, so the 1N5817 is no longer in the power path. **The pigtail itself could still be soldered the wrong way round**, so before first power-up check polarity with a meter (bench check 6): with the supply plugged in, the wire that reads +5.26 V relative to the other is +5 V. Label it before cutting or soldering. This also removes the earlier 1117 dropout concern: about 5.1–5.2 V reaches the S3's 5V pin under load (after the cable, switch and wiring), well above the ≈4.4 V the 1117-3.3 needs at Wi-Fi peaks. The P-MOSFET option is no longer needed. The 1N5817s are kept as spares.
- **The S3 board's own USB-C ports (Q28, unchanged):** programming and debugging only, with the case open; not connected in normal use; not routed through the power switch.
- **Both connected at once** (programming or debugging while the modem's own supply is on): the main 5 V rail and the PC's USB both reach the S3's 5V net. The S3's VBUS diodes (bench check 1) stop the modem's rail pushing current back into the PC's USB port. If check 1 shows there is no diode, either don't power the modem from its own supply while the S3's USB is connected, or put a spare 1N5817 between the main 5 V rail and the S3's 5V pin (the S3 would then see ≈4.8 V, still enough for the 1117).
- *Proposed:* bulk capacitance **470 µF 16 V** (Surplustronics) on the 5 V rail after the power switch, for Wi-Fi and amp current peaks. (It exceeds the 10 µF the USB spec allows at plug-in, but it is only connected when the switch is turned on; USB chargers tolerate this in practice.)
- 5 V feeds the ESP32 board's 5 V pin and the MAX98357A (5.26 V is within its 5.5 V maximum). 3.3 V from the ESP32 board's regulator feeds the MAX3232s and LEDs.
- Power switch per 4.2, between the USB-C power socket's VBUS and the 5 V rail. When the S3's own USB is plugged in for programming or debugging, it powers the board regardless of the switch.
- *History:* 20–22 Oct the input was a 2.1 mm centre-positive DC jack protected by a series 1N5817 (≈0.4 V drop, ≈0.2 V margin over the 1117's dropout); superseded 23 Oct.

### 6.1 Approach
- **Decided (Jon, 22 Oct, Q30): new purpose-built code base** implementing USR Sportster command behaviour. Zimodem's PPP/SLIP and telnet code is ported in (Apache-2.0: keep its attribution and mark every ported or changed file).
- **Zimodem review (20 Oct 2026, v4.0 README) — background for the decision:**
  - Licence **Apache-2.0** (LICENSE + NOTICE files).
  - Built with Arduino IDE 1.8.10, not PlatformIO. Default baud 1200. Main and debug UARTs are set by `MAIN_UART_NUM` in `pet2asc.h`.
  - PPP and SLIP already run on the ESP32. Both currently exit only by resetting the modem.
  - **Its command set diverges from Hayes/USR:** `ATDT`/`ATDP` are telnet/PETSCII modifiers, not tone/pulse; hosts are quoted (`ATD"host:port"`); `AT&D` sets packet delimiter bytes (DTR handling is `ATS63`); `AT&K` sets flow control (USR uses `&H`/`&R`; `&K` is compression); `ATL` re-sends a packet (not speaker volume); `ATI0`–`ATI10` show Zimodem status; `ATC` and `ATAn` are multi-connection/listener commands; `AT&U` updates firmware from Bo Zimmerman's web site.
  - It also carries many unneeded features: PETSCII, Commodore 1650/1660/1670 emulation, IRC, printing, SSH, SD shell, HTTP file streaming.
  - **File-transfer code (checked 23 Oct):** `proto_xmodem` (532 lines, Bo Zimmerman, Apache-2.0); `proto_zmodem` (≈2,900 lines, derived from the Synchronet/SyncTERM ZMODEM code by Jacques Mattheij, rights transferred to Stephen Hurd in 2015; licence not stated in the file, **accepted by Jon, Q75**); `proto_kermit` (UNIX Kermit, Columbia University 1981–83, adapted by Bo Zimmerman). All three are wired to Zimodem's SD-card shell.
- **Target: ESP32-S3** (board decided, 5.2).
- **Decided (Q26): Arduino-ESP32 core 2.x (2.0.17)** on PlatformIO's `espressif32` 6.x platform. Core 3.x (needs the community "pioarduino" platform) is a later option.
- **Check early:** PPP server and NAT on the S3 bench prototype. Zimodem already runs PPP on core 2.0.17, but NAT, clean hang-up and throughput still need a bench test.

### 6.2 Serial
- **UART allocation (22 Oct):** GPIO43/44 (UART0) are wired to the board's onboard USB-serial chip, so the **host RS232 port uses UART1** on pins from the Electronics pin map. UART0 is the USB maintenance console (6.7). The native USB port (GPIO19/20) is left as USB-Serial/JTAG for debugging and the v2 USB CDC option (Q25).
- **Polarity:** the MAX3232 inverts in both directions, so on the TTL side asserted = low. That matches the ESP32 UART's native active-low RTS/CTS, so UART1's **hardware RTS/CTS flow control is used directly, with no inversion**. DTR (input), DCD and RI (outputs) are plain GPIO, active-low.
- Full hardware handshake: RTS/CTS flow control, DTR, DCD, RI. **DSR is hard-wired asserted** (5.1), so `&S` is fixed at `&S0`.
- **Decided (Q1): host speed and line speed are separate.**
  - Host serial speed is **auto-detected from the "AT" prefix, as on the original modem** (`&B0`, default), with a fixed-rate option (`&B1`, default 38400). The Arduino core's built-in baud detection may not support the S3; if not, detect in software from the timing of the `A`/`a` character. Needs a bench test.
  - Emulated line speed is set with USR-style **`&N`** (`&N0` = default 33600). It selects the handshake sound and the `CONNECT` text.
- **Decided (Q2):** data runs at full serial speed; throttling to the line speed is an option.
- **Decided (Q33):** hardware flow control on (`&H1&R2`), `&D2` (DTR drop hangs up), `&C1` (DCD follows the connection), `&S0` fixed, `+++` escape with 1 s guard time and `ATO` to return.
- **Decided (Q34):** `CONNECT 33600` plain by default; protocol suffix (e.g. `/ARQ`) optional.

### 6.3 Dialling
- Numeric only, so old terminal address books that reject letters still work.
- IP 1.2.3.4 port 789 → `ATDT001002003004789`. The first 12 digits are the zero-padded IP and the rest is the port. **Default port 23** if no port digits are given.
- Strip spaces, dashes, brackets and dial modifiers (`,`, `W`, `@`, `!`, `;`, `T`, `P`).
- `ATDP` behaves like `ATDT` but plays pulse-dial clicks instead of DTMF.
- **Decided rules (Q35–37), implemented in v0.1 (`lib/dialstring`, 23 unit tests):**
  - 12 digits = IP on port 23; 13–17 digits = IP + port; fewer than 12 = phonebook entry or special number.
  - **Invalid** (octet > 255, port 0 or > 65535, > 17 digits, other letters) → `ERROR` before any sound plays (Q36).
  - **Hostnames:** a dial string in quotes, or containing `.` or `:`, is a host (`ATDT"bbs.example.com:2323"`, `ATDTwww.amigaretro.com`), and modifier stripping is skipped (Q35). *Implementation note (22 Oct):* the "together with letters" condition was dropped, so a dotted IP address (`ATDT170.64.142.64:23`) also works as a host. Neither `.` nor `:` is a Hayes dial modifier, so nothing is lost.
  - **Phonebook:** numbers 1–11 digits, exact match; each entry stores host, port, Telnet/raw mode and optional line speed (Q37).
- **Special numbers (decided 22 Oct):**
  - **`ATDT123`**: the built-in BBS-style configuration screen (6.8).
  - **`ATDT666`: Internet (PPP)** (Q12).
  - **`ATDT667`: SLIP** (Q51).
  - Zimodem's `5517546` (PPP) and `5517545` (SLIP) also work.
  - Reserved numbers can't be used as phonebook entries.
- **Test BBS (decided, Q58):** Amiga Retro, `www.amigaretro.com:23` (170.64.142.64:23; numeric `ATDT170064142064`). **Pre-loaded as phonebook entry `1`**, stored by hostname, so `ATDT1` dials it.
- Wi-Fi is configured by AT commands, the configuration screen (6.8), the USB maintenance console (6.7) and, once the modem is on the network, the web interface (6.11). No captive Wi-Fi setup portal and no Ethernet.

### 6.4 Sounds
- Order: dial tone → DTMF (generated live from the actual digits) → handshake. **No ringback.**
- Handshake sound depends on the emulated speed: **1200, 2400, 14.4k, 28.8k, 33.6k, 56k**.
- Dial tone, DTMF, busy tone, ring and pulse clicks are generated in firmware (no flash used).
- **Decided (Jon, 22 Oct, Q39): UK tones.**
  - Dial tone: 350 + 450 Hz, continuous.
  - Busy: 400 Hz, 0.375 s on / 0.375 s off.
  - Ring (answer mode, 6.6): 400 + 450 Hz, 0.4 s on, 0.2 s off, 0.4 s on, 2.0 s off.
  - Pulse dialling: UK convention, 10 pulses per second, digit *n* = *n* pulses, 0 = 10 pulses.
  - DTMF is the same worldwide.
- Handshakes stored in LittleFS as **plain 16-bit PCM WAV, 8 kHz mono** (≈16 KB/s, under 1 MB for all six), played over I2S (Q41).
- **Decided (Q40):** with the speaker off (`ATM0`), connect immediately with no silent handshake delay.
- **Decided (Q11): Claude sources freely licensed handshake recordings** for each speed and records source and licence in `credits/LICENSES.md` (section 8). Existing files in `sound/`: `dial-up.wav` (15 s, 48 kHz, stereo, 16-bit) and `busy-signal.wav` (4.8 s, 44.1 kHz, stereo), both from the RetroExternalModem repo (GPL-3.0).
- **Decided (Q9):** speaker commands `ATM0/1/2` and `ATL0–3`. The physical pot sets overall volume and `ATL` scales within it; the hard volume cap (5.3) applies last.
- Drive the amp SD pin low when no sound is playing (5.3).

### 6.6 Other features (decided 22 Oct)
- **Telnet and raw TCP (Q38):** Telnet by default, overridable per phonebook entry. Telnet handles option negotiation and 0xFF escaping, negotiates BINARY both ways (for file transfers), reports terminal type `ANSI` and window size 80×24. Raw mode passes bytes unchanged.
- **Answer mode (Q14):** listen on **port 23**, **no password**; raise RI, play the UK ring, support `ATA` / `ATS0` auto-answer.
- **Remote caller isolation (Q44):** a remote caller can never reach command mode, the configuration screen or any `AT*` command.
- **Internet dial-up (Q13, Q42):** PPP on `ATDT666` and SLIP on `ATDT667`, for Windows 9x / Amiga TCP stacks. Any username and password accepted. Own subnet (default 10.64.64.1 modem / 10.64.64.2 client, configurable) with NAT to Wi-Fi. DTR drop or LCP terminate ends the session and drops DCD.
- **`ATI` identity:** mimic a USR Sportster 33.6 Faxmodem so old operating systems detect it. Research the authentic `ATI0`–`ATI7` responses. **Decided (Q55):** `ATI0`–`ATI7` stay authentic; the firmware version is shown in the boot banner, `AT*VER` and the About screen.
- **Fax (Q32):** `AT+FCLASS=0` → `OK`; fax classes → `ERROR`.
- **Command conventions (Q31):** standard commands follow USR Sportster behaviour; project-specific commands use **`AT*`** (6.8).
- **OTA updates (Jon, Q15):** ArduinoOTA (PlatformIO `espota` upload), password-protected, local network only. No update checks over the internet.
  - **OTA listener on by default.** A setting switches it to **on-demand mode**: off normally, opened for 10 minutes by an `AT*` command from the serial side or the console.
  - ~~Q52: no built-in default password; OTA refuses uploads until a password is set.~~ **Superseded 23 Oct by Q62:** OTA uses the **device password** below, which always exists.
  - Note: the ArduinoOTA password check is a weak MD5 challenge and the image is not encrypted in transit. Acceptable on the home LAN.
- **Device password (decided, Jon 23 Oct, Q62, Q69, Q70)** — one login for the web interface (6.11) and OTA:
  - **Username `admin`.**
  - **Password: 12 random characters generated at first boot** and printed on the USB maintenance console (6.7).
  - **Shown at every boot until it is first used** (a web login or OTA upload) **or changed** (Q69). After that only hashes are kept, so it can't be shown again; `AT*PASS` replaces it.
  - **`AT*PASS` (Q70):** changes it **without asking for the old password**.
    - `AT*PASS` alone generates a new random password and shows it once.
    - `AT*PASS=<text>` sets your own: 8–32 printable characters.
    - Allowed from the USB console and the RS232 port, and from the logged-in web page. Never from a remote caller (Q44).
  - *Implementation notes (22–23 Oct):*
    - Generate from the hardware RNG with its entropy source enabled (`esp_random()` is only truly random while the RF is on, so generate after Wi-Fi starts or call `bootloader_random_enable()` first).
    - Alphabet without look-alike characters (no 0/O, 1/l/I): ≈56 symbols, so 12 characters ≈ 70 bits.
    - Never written to the log buffer, `AT*LOG`, config export or the web page.
    - Until first use it is kept in NVS so it can be reprinted at boot. After first use only hashes are stored: the HTTP Digest key MD5(`admin:usr-modem:password`) with a fixed realm `usr-modem` (so renaming the mDNS hostname doesn't break it), and MD5(password) for ArduinoOTA (`setPasswordHash`). MD5 is weak but both protocols require it.
    - `AT*FACTORY` erases it; the next boot counts as a first boot and prints a new one.
- **Wi-Fi (Q43):** one saved network, optional static IP. mDNS name **`usr-modem`** (`usr-modem.local`) by default, **configurable**. Lost Wi-Fi during a call → `NO CARRIER`.
- **Credential storage (Q46):** Wi-Fi password in NVS without flash encryption. It is **never displayed, logged or exported**; screens show only "set" / "not set".
- **USB CDC serial (Q25): kept as a viable v2 feature** (Jon expects to add it in version 2). The native USB port stays free for it. *Case implication for v2 only:* needs a USB opening.

### 6.7 USB maintenance console and factory reset (decided 22 Oct, Q47–48)
- Console on **UART0 through the onboard USB-serial chip** (the USB-C port also used for flashing), fixed 115200 8N1, independent of every host-serial setting. Build with `ARDUINO_USB_CDC_ON_BOOT=0` so Arduino `Serial` is UART0.
- At boot it prints firmware version, Wi-Fi state and IP, OTA state, and the host-serial settings (speed and `&B` mode, flow control, echo, quiet, verbose).
- **Device password (decided 23 Oct, Q62, Q69):** the boot banner also prints the web/OTA login, `admin` and the generated device password (6.6), **at every boot until it is first used or changed**. It is printed only on the console, never on the RS232 port.
- It accepts the full AT and `AT*` command set, on the same stored configuration, including the configuration screen (`AT*CONFIG`). Dialling from the console is allowed while the RS232 side is idle (bench testing). Debug logging is switchable.
- **Factory reset:**
  - `AT&F` — Hayes behaviour: factory modem settings into the active configuration (`AT&W` saves them). Wi-Fi, phonebook and device password are kept.
  - `AT*FACTORY` — erase all stored settings including Wi-Fi, phonebook and device password, after a Y/N confirmation, then reboot. Accepted on the console and the RS232 port. The reboot generates and prints a new device password.
- No console password: access requires opening the case.
- **v0.1 stub:** boot banner (version, commit, flash, PSRAM, reset reason) and `AT`, `AT*VER`, `AT*DIALTEST <dial string>` (6.12).
- **v0.3:** adds `AT*HELP` / `AT*?` and the `AT*HW…` hardware tests (6.14); the banner names the reset reason (e.g. "power-on", "brown-out").

### 6.11 Web interface (decided 23 Oct, Q61–64, Q71, Q72; page built in v0.2)
- **Decided (Q61): included.** On by default; an `AT*` setting turns it off. The page itself is built (v0.2, PR #2); the firmware's web server comes after the AT parser and menu model (6.8).
- **Resources:** a web server and update handler add ≈150–250 KB to a 4 MB app slot (estimate). The page files are **≈127 KB gzipped** in LittleFS (xterm.js ≈88 KB, two font weights ≈30 KB, the page ≈10 KB). RAM: a few tens of KB per connection, against 512 KB SRAM + 8 MB PSRAM. Wi-Fi and the web server run on core 0; the modem data path and audio stay on core 1.
- **Decided (Q62): login `admin` + the device password** (6.6), the same password as OTA.
- **Decided (Q63): HTTP only** (no HTTPS option).
  - Protection: **Digest authentication** (the password itself is never sent), **CSRF tokens** on every change, and a **Host-header check** that only accepts `usr-modem.local` (or the configured name) and the modem's IP, which also blocks DNS rebinding. The page also sets a Content-Security-Policy that allows only the modem's own scripts.
  - Accepted risk: the session isn't encrypted, so pages and settings can be read by someone sniffing the home network. The Wi-Fi password is a write-only field and never sent to the browser.
  - The WebSocket can't use Digest, so after the Digest-authenticated page load the page fetches a single-use token (`GET /api/session`, valid 30 s) and presents it when opening the WebSocket.
  - **Decided (Q72): login required for every page**, including status.
- **Decided (Q64): browser firmware upload** of `firmware.bin` (and the LittleFS image), streamed into the inactive app slot with the ESP32 `Update` library, then reboot.
  - Only images built for this project (project name embedded in the image is checked).
  - Refused during a call.
  - Downgrades allowed after a confirmation.
  - Browser uploads bypass the PlatformIO test gate (6.10); use the images from a GitHub release, which are only built after the tests pass. Releases now include `littlefs.bin` (v0.2).
  - A LittleFS image upload replaces the sounds and the web files together (they share the partition).
- **Decided (Jon, 23 Oct): Wi-Fi changes from the web page are allowed**, with automatic revert to the previous network if the new one doesn't connect within 60 s.
- The 22 Oct "no web portal" decision was about Wi-Fi setup before the modem is on the network; that stays AT/console-only.
- Web pages run on port 80, separate from answer mode on port 23, so remote-caller isolation (Q44) is unaffected.
- **Page design (Jon, 23 Oct, late: no graphics will be supplied; build a simple, small page like MobaXterm's window):** built in v0.2.
  - **Main area:** **decided (Q71) a real terminal** (xterm.js 6.0.0), fixed at **80 × 25** and scaled to fill the window, on a **black background**. Font **IBM Plex Mono** (OFL-1.1). Classic **VGA 16-colour palette**, as an ANSI BBS looked on a PC. Keyboard-driven, like the serial screen. It shows the same configuration screen as `AT*CONFIG`.
  - **One status bar along the bottom**, MobaXterm style, with dividers and small icons:
    - **Live front-panel lights** AA, CD, RD, SD, TR, CS, ARQ/FAX: small red rectangular LEDs with labels, matching the real modem.
    - **Connection status:** Idle, Dialling, Ringing, `Connected · Telnet · 33600`, `Connected (incoming)`, `Internet (PPP)` / `(SLIP)`, Config screen, Client test. Green when connected, yellow while busy.
    - **Remote host and IP:** e.g. `www.amigaretro.com (170.64.142.64:23)`.
    - **Connection time** (hh:mm:ss).
    - **Modem Wi-Fi** network and signal (an addition).
    - **Modem clock**, in the modem's time zone, right-aligned.
    - **Page ↔ modem link** (Online / Offline / Connecting), with automatic reconnect (1, 2, 5, then every 10 s).
    - On narrow windows the bar drops Wi-Fi first, then the remote host, then the connection time, then the clock.
  - **Real-time LEDs, resource check (23 Oct): cheap.** The firmware sends a 1-byte LED mask to each open page only when the LEDs change, at most 20 times a second (well under 1 KB/s). RD/SD are already "stretched" on the physical modem (5.4), so the web copies the same flashes, not individual bytes. Expect ≈50–150 ms lag over Wi-Fi. The page's own Wi-Fi traffic doesn't light RD/SD.
  - **Protocol** (`web/PROTOCOL.md`): one WebSocket `/ws`. Binary frames carry terminal bytes both ways (UTF-8 + ANSI). JSON text frames from the modem: `hello` (name, version, CSRF token), `led` (bit mask), `clock` (UTC epoch + offset), `call` (state, mode, direction, number, host, IP, port, speed, start time), `wifi` (state, SSID, RSSI, IP). Unknown messages are ignored, so newer firmware won't break older pages.
  - **Demo mode** (`index.html?demo`) and a **single-file preview** (`scripts/make_preview.py` → `web/preview.html`, also attached to every CI run) simulate the modem: **D** fakes a call to phonebook entry 1, **H** hangs up. The demo's menu is a mock-up, not the final screen.
  - Superseded: the background-graphic design, Jon-supplied graphics, and the VGA bitmap font (VileR) proposed on 23 Oct.

### 6.13 Client Tests (requirement, Jon 23 Oct; extras decided Q73–75)
A **Client Tests** section in the configuration screen (6.8) that checks what the connected computer and terminal program can do. Every test also has an `AT*` command (parity rule, 6.8), e.g. `AT*TEST SIZE`, `AT*TEST KEYS`, `AT*SZ <file>`, `AT*SX <file>`, `AT*RZ`.
- **Required (Jon):**
  - **Display tests:** ANSI, PC and Plain output.
    - ANSI: 16 foreground and background colours, bright/bold, underline, reverse, blink, cursor movement, clear screen/line.
    - PC: the full CP437 table, box-drawing and block-shading characters.
    - Plain: 7-bit ASCII only.
    - Also an ISO-8859-1 (Latin-1) table, the Amiga's native character set, so the user can see which set their terminal uses (Q73).
  - **ZMODEM download** and **XMODEM download**, using the log file or a sound file.
    - The log is a snapshot of the PSRAM ring buffer, sent as `USRMODEM.LOG`.
    - Sound files are sent from LittleFS with 8.3 DOS-safe names (e.g. `HS33600.WAV`).
    - XMODEM in checksum, CRC and 1K variants.
    - Report bytes, time, characters per second and retries.
  - **Screen width and height measurement:** move the cursor to row 999, column 999 and ask the terminal where it actually is (ANSI cursor-position report, `ESC[6n`). If there's no reply within ≈1 s, show a ruler and ask the user to type the last column and row they can see.
- **Additions (decided, Jon 23 Oct, Q73):**
  - **YMODEM (batch) download**, which also sends the file name and size (supported by most Amiga and DOS terminals).
  - **A generated test file** of a chosen size (e.g. 64 KB or 1 MB, `TEST1M.BIN`) for throughput testing.
  - **Uploads:** the user sends any file by X/Y/ZMODEM; the modem checks it, reports throughput and errors, and discards it (nothing is stored).
  - **Terminal identification:** answerback (ENQ), Device Attributes (`ESC[c`) and Device Status (`ESC[5n`) replies, shown as received.
  - **Keyboard test:** shows the code each key sends (Backspace 08 vs 7F, Delete, arrows, function keys, Enter as CR or CR LF). Ends after a set key or a timeout.
  - **Line endings and echo:** shows whether the terminal adds its own line feeds (stair-stepping) and whether local echo is on (doubled characters).
  - **Throughput and flow control:** the modem sends data flat out for a set time and reports the rate and how often the computer paused it with RTS (shows hardware flow control works). A matching receive test.
  - **Handshake signals and cable check:** live display of DTR and RTS from the computer, and the modem toggles DCD, RI and CTS in turn so the user can watch the terminal's indicators. This finds missing wires in a cable. DSR shows as always on (hard-wired, 5.1).
  - **Bell:** sends BEL so the user can hear whether the terminal beeps.
  - **Serial format:** shows the detected host speed and format (e.g. 38400 8N1).
  - **Summary and apply (Q74):** a results page, with an offer (and confirmation) to save the findings (character set, screen size, Backspace key) as this client's settings so the menus adapt.
- **Decided (Q73):** Client Tests run only on serial sessions (RS232 port and USB console). The web interface lists them but can't run them, because they test the serial client.
- **Decided (Q75): transfer code from Zimodem.** XMODEM/YMODEM from Zimodem's `proto_xmodem` (Apache-2.0); ZMODEM from Zimodem's `proto_zmodem` (Synchronet/SyncTERM origin, no licence stated in the file; **accepted by Jon**). Keep the original headers and mark changes. Kermit is not included.

### 6.14 Hardware tests (requirement, Jon 24 Oct; built in v0.3, PR #3)
Tests for each part of the electronics as it is built. From v0.3 they are `AT*HW…` commands on the USB maintenance console. Later they also become a **Hardware tests** screen in the configuration menu (6.8, parity rule).
- **Guided test `AT*HWTEST`.** Six steps, each with a prompt (Enter = start, S = skip, Q = quit), ending in a pass/fail summary:
  1. **LEDs:** each LED lights for 1 s, left to right, with its name and GPIO printed. The user confirms.
  2. **Speaker:** 1 kHz, a 200 Hz–4 kHz sweep, UK dial tone, DTMF 0–9 * #, UK pulse clicks, busy tone.
  3. **Volume cap:** 1 kHz and the sweep at the firmware maximum (the −7 dB cap, 5.3). Listen for distortion, buzz or rattle. This is the bench check of the cap.
  4. **Pot:** live reading (raw, mV, level %, dB), with a 1 kHz tone following the knob. Passes if both ends are reached (0 % and 100 %) and the user confirms.
  5. **RS232 lines:** live DTR and RTS from the computer. Keys D / R / C toggle DCD, RI and CTS while the user watches the terminal program's indicators.
  6. **Loopback:** with a loopback plug fitted, data at 300–230,400 baud, then CTS → RTS and DCD → DTR. Pass/fail is automatic.
- **Single tests:**

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `AT*HW?` | List the hardware-test commands (`AT*HELP` lists everything) |
| `AT*HWPINS` | Print the GPIO pin map |
| `AT*HWINFO` | Chip, MAC, flash, PSRAM, heap, firmware, reset reason, uptime, LittleFS use |
| `AT*HWFILES` | List the LittleFS files (checks the sounds/web upload) |
| `AT*HWLED=<AA…ARQ\|ALL\|OFF\|CHASE\|WALK>` | Light one LED, all, none, a sweep, or each in turn with its name |
| `AT*HWAMP=<AUTO\|ON\|OFF>` | Amp SD pin: AUTO = on only while a sound plays (default); OFF mutes while I2S keeps running (for a scope) |
| `AT*HWVOL=<0–100\|POT>` | Test volume as % of the cap on the audio curve (default 75), or follow the pot |
| `AT*HWTONE=<Hz>[,<ms>]`, `=DIAL`, `=BUSY`, `=RING`, `=SWEEP` | Test tone 20–7,000 Hz, or a UK tone / sweep until a key is pressed |
| `AT*HWDTMF=<digits>`, `AT*HWPULSE=<digits>` | DTMF, or UK pulse-dial clicks |
| `AT*HWPOT[=TONE]` | Live pot reading, optionally with a tone that follows it |
| `AT*HWLINES` | Live DTR/RTS; toggle DCD/RI/CTS |
| `AT*HWSIG=<DCD\|RI\|CTS>,<ON\|OFF>` | Hold one output on or off (for a meter on the DB25) |
| `AT*HWLOOP[=<baud>\|ALL]` | Loopback test (plug required) |
| `AT*HWBRIDGE[=<baud>]` | Console ↔ RS232 pass-through (default 38400, CTS and DCD on, Ctrl-X ends): type in a PC terminal on the Unitek COM port and see it on the console, and the reverse |

- **Loopback plug:** join RXD–TXD, CTS–RTS and DCD–DTR (DB25 pins 3–2, 5–4, 8–20; bench DB9 pins 2–3, 8–7, 1–4). RI has no spare input to loop back to: check it with `AT*HWLINES` and the terminal's RI indicator, or `AT*HWSIG=RI,ON` and a meter on DB25 22.
- **Safe on a bare or part-built board:** no pin is driven until a test uses it. The pin map is one file, `lib/hw/src/pins.h` (*proposed*, section 7, Q76). `AT*HWPINS` prints it, and a unit test checks it against the S3's reserved pins before every upload.
- The volume cap always applies last, whatever `AT*HWVOL` says.
- **Where they run (decided, Jon 24 Oct, Q79):** a **Hardware tests** item in the configuration screen on every local session, plus the `AT*HW…` commands.
  - **USB console and web page:** all tests. The RS232 line, loopback and bridge tests run only while the modem is idle (no call, no RS232 session in progress). On the web page the bridge shows the RS232 traffic in the browser terminal, and the line test shows DTR/RTS live with on-screen toggles for DCD, RI and CTS.
  - **RS232 session** (`AT*CONFIG` / `ATDT123` on the host port): everything except the line, loopback and bridge tests, which are listed as unavailable with the reason (they would take over the port the session is using).
  - **Remote caller (answer mode):** none (Q44).
  - Supersedes the 24 Oct proposal that limited the line, loopback and bridge tests to the USB console.
- **Not yet:** WAV playback from LittleFS and the Wi-Fi signal test come with the Sounds screen and Wi-Fi (6.4, 6.8).

- **Electronics → Case:** the final materials list (section 3.1, decided 22 Oct). Measured heights, S3 size, header row spacing (25.4 mm) and PC2008 holes (Ø3.3 mm, centres ≈3.25 mm in) are in sections 3.1, 3.2 and 4.4. Still to supply: assembled S3 stack height.

GPIO43/44 are the onboard USB-serial (UART0), so the host port uses other pins (22 Oct).
- **Firmware → Electronics:** flash size needed (app size × 2 for OTA, plus sound storage). With the S3 N16R8 (16 MB) this is no longer expected to constrain the board choice. *(22 Oct)* Host RS232 is on **UART1**: TXD, RXD, RTS and CTS can go on any usable GPIO, and RTS/CTS need no extra inversion (6.2). UART0 (GPIO43/44) is the maintenance console; nothing else on those pins. Debugging uses the native USB port (GPIO19/20) via built-in USB-JTAG; keep those pins free. The web interface (6.11) and Client Tests (6.13) need no hardware change.
- **Firmware → Electronics: proposed GPIO pin map (24 Oct; Electronics to confirm, Q76).** v0.3's hardware tests use it (`lib/hw/src/pins.h`). Changing it is a one-file edit, so Electronics can move pins to suit the board layout. It uses only the usable pins in 5.2 and groups each lead into one run of header pins:

| Function | GPIO | S3 header (5.2) | Goes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| RXD out (UART1 TX) | 4 | Left, pins 4–10 (one run of 7, after RST) | MAX3232 driver → DB25 3 |
| TXD in (UART1 RX) | 5 | ″ | MAX3232 receiver ← DB25 2 |
| CTS out (UART1 RTS) | 6 | ″ | driver → DB25 5 |
| RTS in (UART1 CTS) | 7 | ″ | receiver ← DB25 4 |
| DTR in | 15 | ″ | receiver ← DB25 20 |
| DCD out | 16 | ″ | driver → DB25 8 |
| RI out | 17 | ″ | driver → DB25 22 |
| I2S BCLK, LRC, DIN | 11, 12, 13 | Left, pins 17–19 | MAX98357A |
| Amp SD (HIGH = on) | 14 | Left, pin 20, then 5V and GND | MAX98357A: **the 6-wire amp lead comes from 6 consecutive pins** |
| Pot wiper (ADC1) | 1 | Right, pin 4 | WH148 wiper + 100 nF |
| LEDs AA, CD, RD, SD, TR | 42, 41, 40, 39, 38 | Right, pins 6–10 | Resistor → LED → GND (HIGH = lit) |
| LEDs CS, ARQ | 48, 21 | Right, pins 16, 18 | ″ |
| Spare | 2 (ADC1), 8, 9 (ADC1), 10 (ADC1), 18 | — | — |

  - Polarity as in 6.2: TTL side asserted = LOW. Which MAX3232 channel carries which signal is a schematic detail; firmware doesn't care.
  - LED drive: GPIO sources the current (GPIO → resistor → LED → GND). If Electronics wires them to 3.3 V instead (GPIO sinks), firmware flips one constant (`kLedActiveHigh`).
  - GPIO39–42 are the JTAG pins; free because debugging uses USB-JTAG (5.2).
- **Firmware → Electronics: boot-state resistors (24 Oct, *proposed*, Q77).** From reset until the firmware sets the pins (and throughout flashing), the S3's GPIOs float.
  - **10 kΩ pull-ups to 3.3 V on the four MAX3232 driver inputs** (RXD, CTS, DCD, RI on the TTL side). The computer then sees idle and "off" instead of random levels, so a terminal doesn't get garbage or a false carrier while the modem boots.
  - **10 kΩ pull-down on the amp's SD pin**, so the amp stays off (no pop or hiss) until the firmware turns it on. Breakout modules like this usually pull SD up to Vin through ≈1 MΩ (check the TradeMe module); with 10 kΩ down, SD sits at ≈0.05 V, below the MAX98357A's 0.16 V shutdown threshold. Driven HIGH (3.3 V), it selects the left channel. Firmware sends the same audio on both channels, so any SD level that isn't shutdown plays it.
  - Cost: 5 resistors from Jon's stock.
- **Firmware → Case (v2 only):** USB CDC serial (Q25) would need a USB opening in the case. No change for v1.
- **Case → Electronics:** rear-panel layout (DB25 centred on the split, 1 × RJ11, **USB-C power socket in place of the DC jack, 23 Oct**), M2.5 hardware only, no USB access hole, clear internal height 29.0 mm (≈20 mm under the front LED shelf), existing 5.1 × 2.2 mm LED windows (suit ZD0230), switch window unchanged (SW0133 fits), volume pot pocket unchanged (WH148 fits), speaker mount to be modified for the BZ0512, standoff positions for the 80 × 120 mm board (with pot notch). **Layout check, 22 Oct (4.4):** the board needs a second notch ≈6 × 11 mm at the front-right corner for the switch; the rear connectors clash with the board's rear strip unless the standoffs are ≈3 mm and the strip is kept clear, or the board is cut to ≈68 mm deep (Q50); the S3 must not sit under the speaker unless the speaker moves. **Q67 check, 23 Oct, night (4.4):** the brown 50 × 100 board can't lie flat beside any white board; **two white ½ Perma-Protos side by side fit** (S3 board on the left, RS232/power on the right, speaker unmoved); both run under the rear connectors, so ≈3 mm standoffs and a clear ≈10 mm rear strip on each; amp *proposed* on the floor at the left end.
